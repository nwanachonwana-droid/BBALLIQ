How the board is built

Three findings decide what makes it on, and one of them ruled a whole market out. All written up in full below.

01  /  WHICH PROP MARKETS

Minutes can be forecast. Hot nights can't.

A model predicts one thing honestly about a player: how long he's on the floor. That falls out of the spread, the rotation and blowout risk. Everything past it is a guy getting hot. Combination markets win twice — stack three stats and the random parts cancel while the minutes part compounds. We scored every market on that ratio across 362 rotation players and three seasons.

02  /  WHICH COLLEGE GAMES

Under 60% confidence, nobody knows anything.

We split 1,544 college games by how sure the model was and checked what happened. The split was sharper than expected, and it held for the betting market too.

UNDER 60% SURE
56.8%

Barely better than guessing. The market did worse — 50.4%, a coin flip. Nothing from this half goes out.

60% SURE OR BETTER
78.2%

A 21-point jump, same model, same season. On a typical slate that's two to four games.

03  /  WHY WE DON'T OFFER EUROLEAGUE TOTALS

The same edge is worth less on a total than on the winner.

We measured how far EuroLeague results land from our projections, across 310 games, always forecasting games the model had not seen. Margins behaved. Totals did not.

HOW WRONG WE ARE ON MARGIN
11.7 pts

Typical miss on the winning margin. Close to what we expected before measuring, which is the boring outcome you want.

HOW WRONG WE ARE ON THE TOTAL
18.1 pts

Half again as wide, and 51% worse than we had assumed going in. That's 10.4% of a EuroLeague total against 6.0% of an NBA one.

WHAT A 3-POINT EDGE IS ACTUALLY WORTH  ·  AGAINST A 5.4¢ COST TO TRADE
On who wins 10.2¢
On the total 6.6¢
Cost to get in and out 5.4¢
1.2¢

is all that's left on a EuroLeague total after costs — and a 2-point edge leaves nothing at all. On the game winner the same read keeps nearly five cents.

So in EuroLeague we work the margin — who wins, and by how much — and a total has to disagree with the market by a wide mark before it is worth touching. We had assumed totals would be the easier side. The measurement said otherwise.

04  /  WHAT WE HAVEN'T PROVED YET

There is no track record on this site, and there won't be for a while.

Two reasons, and neither is modesty.

A WIN RATE WOULD BE NOISE

Telling a 53% edge from a coin flip takes on the order of 750 settled bets. Anything posted before then says more about luck than about method, and a good first month would be the most misleading version of it.

What we track instead is the price we got against the price the market closed at. That settles in roughly 30 bets, because it measures whether the money agreed with us rather than whether the ball went in. When there are thirty, it goes on the site — good or bad.

EARLY RATINGS ARE MOSTLY LAST YEAR

Six games is not enough to rate a team. Take them at face value and the best side looks better than it is and the worst looks worse, so every projected margin comes out too wide — in the direction that loses money fastest, overconfidence on the extremes.

So a rating this early is part this season and part last, weighted by how much has actually been played. In EuroLeague right now that is about 59% this season. It reaches 80% by around Christmas and the correction fades on its own. That is why the board looks cautious in October, and why it should.

LIMITS

What this doesn't tell you.

A board is not a bet

It says whose numbers are forecastable, not that the market has them wrong.

Accuracy is not profit

We measured how often we pick the right team, not what it earns. 78% on heavy favorites still loses at a bad price.

Short runs tell you nothing

A 78% model loses four straight regularly. That's variance, not a broken model.

One season of evidence

Every figure here comes from 2025–26. Markets adapt and models stop working. We'll publish what happens either way.
